What a verdict looks like

A real one, so you know what you’re getting before you paste anything.

Out of scope

The agreement covers three rounds of revisions on the agreed page set, and the client has already used all three. A fourth round is new work, even though revisions themselves are covered.

“Includes up to three (3) rounds of revisions across the five agreed page designs. Additional rounds are quoted separately.”
Reply to the client

Happy to take another look at the pricing page — I want it to land properly too. One thing worth flagging: our agreement covers three rounds of revisions, and we wrapped the third one last week, so this would be a fourth. I can pick it up as a small add-on with its own timeline and cost, or roll it into the next phase if you’d rather keep everything together. Let me know which you prefer and I’ll send the details across.

Worth pinning down

What this catches

  • Extras dressed up as small favours “While you’re in there” is a scope change with a friendlier face.
  • Revision rounds past the limit The topic is covered. The count isn’t. Those are different questions, and only one of them is in your contract.
  • Wording that was always going to be argued about Some terms genuinely read two ways. Better to find that out now than halfway through the work.

About your contract

You’re pasting a client agreement into a stranger’s website. That deserves a straight answer rather than a link to a policy.

The text you paste is used for your check and nothing else. It is not saved to our database, it is not written to our logs, and it is not used to train anything. What we keep is your email address, the verdict, how confident it was, and how long your inputs were — enough to know whether the tool works, and not enough to read your contract.
